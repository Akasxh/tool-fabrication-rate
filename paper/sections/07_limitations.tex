\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\label{sec:limitations:rvr-null}

A per-tier operator recommendation is condensed in
Table~\ref{tab:deployer} (appendix); the rest of this section states what
the audit does and does not license.

We cannot disentangle the Anthropic--Qwen3 gap: parameter count, training recipe, and provider guardrails co-vary, and commercial-vs-open bundles serving path with weights (Section~\ref{sec:results:scaling}). Quantization is no confound: the precision ladder (Section~\ref{sec:results:controls}) shows fabrication persists at full precision. The Anthropic null is also indistinguishable from BFCL v4 contamination (v4 predates every model tested; we ran no membership-inference control). RVR's $20\!\to\!0$ is quantization-invariant ($\mathrm{C}_0$ and $\mathrm{C}_1$ share weights); on Anthropic it is a call-path no-op yet strict-pass drops (Section~\ref{sec:results:rvr}), and our TOST is underpowered at $N\!=\!60$, so we recommend $\mathrm{C}_{0.7}$/RVR only on tiers with audited $\mathrm{TFR}\!>\!0$, paired with executor-side allow-listing. Scope: the probe tests registry-shape, not runtime-shape; TFR ignores latency, abstention, and pass-rate; and we claim no scaling law. With only two non-Qwen3 lineages (Llama, Qwen2.5), we claim no general cross-family transfer. A second benchmark bounds the reach: on $\tau$-bench (simulated user) the commercial null holds ($0/2{,}244$ over gpt-4.1, Sonnet~4.6, Haiku~4.5, $\leq\!0.16\%$), but our probe drew no open-weight fabrication either: Qwen3-8B and 14B made $191$ valid in-registry calls at zero ($\leq\!1.91\%$) while completing none of the $24$ probed $\tau$ tasks. The null may then reflect too little engagement, not robustness, so we read $\tau$ as scoping the open-weight gap to BFCL-style target-removed registries, not as a negative result.
