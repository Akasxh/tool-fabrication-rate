\section{What recovers: content or format}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Does recovery use the \emph{content} of the echoed registry, or only the
\emph{format} of a structured rejection? The condition ladder speaks to
this, but only bounds the effect, because every arm is zero-event.

\paragraph{The ablation is suggestive, not decisive.}
On Qwen3-8B the structured error $\mathrm{C}_{0.7}$, a
\texttt{tool\_not\_found} envelope with \emph{no registry attached}, also
drives fabrications to zero, matching full RVR
(Section~\ref{sec:results:rvr}). If the model needed to \emph{read} the
registry to recover, removing it might cost something; within this
experiment's resolution we see no such cost.

\paragraph{The decoy arm sharpens, but does not close, the question.}
$\mathrm{C}_{0.7}$ removes the list; it does not test a list present but
\emph{wrong}. A registry-as-cue account predicts a decoy list (formatted,
count-matched, populated with non-existent tools) fails to recover, with
no valid name to copy; a format-only account predicts it recovers anyway,
since ``your last call was rejected; choose from this set'' need not be
truthful to suppress the call. The $\mathrm{C}_{0.8}$ condition runs this
contrast, holding the $\mathrm{C}_1$ envelope prose verbatim and swapping
only the listed names for guaranteed-absent decoys.

The decoy list logs $0/410$ (CP 95\% upper bound $\leq\!0.90\%$;
near\_name $0/97$, synonym $0/106$, matched\_random $0/107$, unrelated
$0/100$), matching the no-list envelope ($\mathrm{C}_{0.7}$, $0/448$) and
the real list ($\mathrm{C}_1$, $0/258$ on 8B). All three are zero-event,
so we \emph{detect no content effect at this sample size}: absence of
evidence, not evidence of equivalence. The
$\mathrm{C}_1\!-\!\mathrm{C}_{0.7}$ and $\mathrm{C}_1\!-\!\mathrm{C}_{0.8}$
differences are bounded by a Newcombe 95\% CI of $[-1.5, +1.4]$~pp,
consistent with the registry content buying up to $\sim$1~pp of recovery,
not nothing. The data are thus \emph{compatible with} format-over-content,
the model responding to the message-level rejection rather than the valid
names offered, but we do not treat zero-vs-zero as proof, and a
registry-as-cue account is not ruled out within the $\pm$1.5~pp band.

We settle it on a high-event cell. Qwen2.5-7B opens at
$\mathrm{C}_0=6.10\%$ ($28/459$), and the recovery ladder
(Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:qwen25-ablation}) walks it down to $0.31\%$ at
$\mathrm{C}_1$. All three
envelope arms collapse the rate far below $\mathrm{C}_0$ (Fisher,
$p<10^{-3}$) and none differs from another (pairwise Fisher $p>0.37$);
the \emph{wrong}-list arm matches the real-list arm exactly
(Fisher $p=1.0$). With events to recover, the content effect left open by
the $\pm$1.5~pp Qwen3-8B bound does not appear: a \emph{powered} null
where a \emph{wrong} registry (or none) recovers as well as the right one.
A bare retry ($\mathrm{C}_{0.5}$) helps but less, leaving a residual
$\sim\!5\times$ the structured arms ($p=0.09$ vs.\ $\mathrm{C}_1$).


\paragraph{What this does not claim.}
Format-sufficiency is about \emph{recovery}, not task success: on TFR
alone RVR matches any structured rejection (a hard reject also yields
$0$), but RVR is \emph{recovery-preserving}, returning the live registry
so the agent re-selects a valid tool rather than aborting. The value of
the \emph{true} registry thus shows up in task-completion rate, not TFR.
And the result holds only on tiers with $\mathrm{C}_0$ fabrications to
suppress (1.7--14B); 0.6B and 32B carry no signal to recover.
